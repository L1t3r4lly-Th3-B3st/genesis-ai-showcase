01 / THE MISSION

From curiosity to engineering knowledge

This project is about learning by building: training small models, exploring inference and research tools, understanding the boundaries of what they can do, and gradually developing the expertise for larger local systems.

01 / MODEL FUNDAMENTALS

Original AI experiments

Experimenting with small neural networks trained from scratch, rather than relying on an external large language model for every response.

02 / INFORMATION QUALITY

Source-aware research

Exploring source-linked knowledge retrieval and cautious treatment of web information, including the limits of search and extraction.

03 / SYSTEMS ENGINEERING

Local AI infrastructure

Studying GPUs, VRAM, operating systems, inference performance, and the engineering tradeoffs of running AI on hardware under the developer's control.

02 / WHERE THINGS STAND

What exists today—and what doesn't

CURRENT EXPERIMENTS

Genesis AI research prototype

  • Small original neural next-token generator with 22,064 learned parameters.
  • Separate 45,173-parameter response-intent classifier.
  • Local conversation memory and export/import of compatible chat records.
  • Source-linked knowledge retrieval with a Wikipedia fallback and optional local SearXNG.
  • Read-only research tools, network restrictions, and unit-test coverage.
IMPORTANT LIMITATIONS

Research prototype, not a frontier model

  • The original neural text model is extremely small and can produce incoherent responses.
  • The system does not match commercial large language models in reasoning or general knowledge.
  • External knowledge retrieval is not proof that an answer is correct.
  • The current research backend runs privately; this public page is a project overview, not a live chatbot.
  • Large-model FP16 inference is a future research goal, not a present capability.
Research principle: Report what works, document what fails, and separate demonstrated capabilities from planned work. Accurate limitations are part of the engineering.
03 / RESEARCH ROADMAP

Building toward a dedicated AI research workstation

Funding and hardware assistance would support staged, hands-on learning and experiments. An ambitious long-term objective is studying extremely large open-weight models in full 16-bit precision; reaching that scale requires infrastructure far beyond a typical personal PC.

PHASE 01 — PLANNED

Hardware foundations

Acquire compatible high-memory GPU hardware and supporting equipment; document power, cooling, software setup, and inference constraints.

PHASE 02 — PLANNED

Repeatable evaluations

Measure speed, memory use, accuracy, uncertainty handling, and source attribution on models that the available hardware can actually run.

PHASE 03 — PLANNED

Community resources

Share technical findings, benchmark methods, educational notes, and selected open tools so other independent learners can reproduce the experiments.

The proposed research activities above are goals, not completed studies or guaranteed deliverables. Hardware scope and milestones will depend on available funding.

04 / COMMUNITY & SUPPORT

Help make independent AI research possible

Genesis AI welcomes conversations about equipment donations, compatible GPUs, hardware guidance, funding support, and educational collaboration. Contributions can support specific research phases even when they don't fund an entire workstation.

All sponsorship arrangements would be agreed directly with the project lead. No grant or sponsorship is represented as awarded here.
